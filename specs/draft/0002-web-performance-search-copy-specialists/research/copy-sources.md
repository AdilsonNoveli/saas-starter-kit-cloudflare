# Copy sources

Provenance labels follow `README.md`. **Results quoted by these authors are case studies reported
by the authors themselves; they show a method worked once for that business, not an effect size
to promise.** The specialist must never turn them into a claim on a Nuxfire page.

## Copyhackers

| Id | Statement (paraphrased) | Mode | URL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | Conversion copywriting is copy that moves the reader to "yes" using voice-of-customer data, frameworks, formulas and persuasion techniques; three phases: research and discovery, writing / wireframing / editing, validation and experimentation. Voice-of-customer data is called foundational ("you can't write great copy without it"), with the caveat that biases can distort its reading. | SUM | https://copyhackers.com/conversion-copywriting-defined/ |
| C2 | Messaging hierarchy = the order in which on-page messages convince the reader. Start from three questions: what is the visitor thinking on arrival, what should they do by the end, how must their thinking change in between. The answer to the third decides what goes on the page and how long it is. | RAW | https://copyhackers.com/2016/06/copywriting-principles-sweatblock/ |
| C3 | The "10/90" layout rule: roughly the top 10 % of the page matches the visitor's stage of awareness and the message that brought them; the rest convinces, answering in order: what do you do; why should I care; do others like me care; how do you do it; how will my life improve; why is it safe to believe you; what now. The author says the order is a starting point, not a law. | RAW | same |
| C4 | Prefer specific over vague: concrete details, real numbers, named things, customer language, one central idea per block, about a sixth-grade reading level. Replace placeholder crossheads with the answer to the question they were asking. | SUM + RAW | https://copyhackers.com/how-to-be-specific/ and the article above |
| C5 | Buttons carry either a **call to value** (before the visitor decided: amplify the value of moving on) or a **call to action** (after they decided: remove friction). Both can appear on one page if the page has a single goal. | RAW | C2 URL |
| C6 | Persuasion techniques such as proof from authorities work on later awareness stages; earlier stages need clarity and relevance first ("clarity is sweep number one"). | RAW + search snippet | C2 URL |
| C7 | Reported results on the source pages (for example a 108 % revenue lift on one e-commerce home page) are the author's case studies. | RAW | C2 URL |

## CXL

| Id | Statement (paraphrased) | Mode | URL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| X1 | Voice-of-customer research (Jennifer Havice; published 2020-06-21, updated 2024-06-17): ask customers what triggered the search, what problem the product lessens, which alternatives they considered and what made them hesitate; mine the answers for needs, pain points and hesitations; count recurring themes; compare with the page to find communication gaps; then test. Reported case: a Learn Visual Studio home page test (+9.2 % course conversions on one variation; +66.3 % clicks on the main CTA in another) — author's case study. | RAW | https://cxl.com/blog/voice-of-customer/ |
| X2 | B2B value proposition (Stefan Maritz; published 2025-05-20, updated 2025-10-30): a customer-first statement of why the product solves a high-priority problem better than the alternatives for a specific buyer; define who it is for and who it is not; list 20-50 real pain points; test each against "valuable, critical, can we exceed expectations"; be specific about context; test continuously. Template: for [buyer] who [problem], [product] is the [category] that [mechanism]; unlike [alternatives], it [difference]. | RAW | https://cxl.com/blog/b2b-value-proposition/ |
| X3 | The same article cites a Bain study (2,300+ B2B decision makers) in which cost ranked low among loyalty drivers, and states that vendors excelling on six or more value elements scored a 60 % higher NPS. Reported by CXL from Bain; **not verified against Bain in this session**. | RAW (secondary) | X2 URL |

## What the three named sites do **not** cover

They are marketing-copy sources. None of the pages read covers button and label conventions inside
an application, empty states, permission messages, transactional email or notification text.
For those, N1, N2 and the project's own conventions apply; further primary sources (for example
GOV.UK and Microsoft style guides) are proposed as task T051 and are **not** claimed here.
